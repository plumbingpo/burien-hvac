Think about the spaces your central system has always struggled with: a garage workshop that bakes by July, an addition the ductwork never reached, a bonus room that runs too warm or too cold, or an older Burien home built without ducts in the first place. Those are exactly the jobs a ductless mini split was made for. There is no ductwork to leak or lose energy, because each wall-mounted head is fed by a line set that passes through a small hole in the wall. Inside the outdoor unit, an inverter compressor modulates to match the load, so the room holds within a fraction of a degree, quietly and on very little power. And when winter comes, it runs as a true cold-climate heat pump.

Many disappointing mini splits were doomed on paper, not on the wall. The classic error is oversizing: a head too big for its room cools in a hurry, shuts off before it pulls out the humidity, and never settles into a steady rhythm. Burien HVAC sizes every zone to its real load instead. On multi-zone systems we also check that the outdoor unit's capacity is balanced across the heads it feeds and that every line set stays within the manufacturer's length and lift limits. Then comes placement. We walk the Burien home with you, weigh wall-mounted, ceiling-cassette, and floor-console options, and put each head where it throws air where you live and still looks right, with outdoor line sets dressed in line-hide rather than left bare.

Our installation process is held to the same standard as our biggest central systems. Nitrogen goes into the line set first to prove it holds pressure, then a vacuum pump pulls it down to a level confirmed with a micron gauge. Flares are torqued and leak-tested one by one, since that joint is the classic failure point. Drainage is set up with real fall or a reliable condensate pump, and the refrigerant charge is adjusted to match the line length. Finally, every zone is commissioned in both modes and you get a clear explanation of how the remote actually works. Why Hire a Professional for Ductless Mini Split Installation?

Mini splits look simple hanging on the wall, and that illusion produces a steady stream of failed installs. The flare connections that join the line set are the system's weak point: under-torqued or badly formed flares weep refrigerant for months, and the first symptom is a head blowing lukewarm air a season later. Skipping the nitrogen pressure test and micron-verified vacuum leaves moisture in a circuit that inverter compressors tolerate poorly. Line sets run past the manufacturer's length and lift tables quietly cost capacity, and a condensate drain without proper fall leaves a water stain the first humid week.

Sizing is even less forgiving with ductless than with central air. The smallest heads on the market are already more than many bedrooms need, so picking one size too big means short cycles, sticky humidity, and a room that never settles. That is why our experienced technicians size every zone on its real load, then back it up with disciplined install work: each flare torqued and leak-tested, the circuit pressure-tested and evacuated to a measured vacuum, charge adjusted for the line length, and every head commissioned in heating and cooling. Handling refrigerant is federally regulated, and the unit needs a correctly sized circuit and disconnect. Done this way, a mini split runs quiet and efficient for a decade and a half with nothing to report.

Maintenance Tips for Ductless Mini Split Installation

Smart maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Put these technician-approved steps to work around your property:

  • Have the indoor blower wheel and coil professionally cleaned before they grow anything
  • Give the outdoor unit open space in every season, free of drifts, debris, and mowing clippings
  • In winter, expect defrost pauses and light steam from the outdoor unit; solid ice means call us
  • Get a yearly professional checkup of charge, flare joints, and drainage while any fix is still small
  • Glance at the condensate drain outlet in summer to confirm it is actually dripping
  • Pull and rinse the indoor filter screens monthly, because they fill up much faster than you would expect
  • Resist turning the system off and on; an inverter is most efficient holding one steady temperature
